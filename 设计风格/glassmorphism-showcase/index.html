06Guidelines

Do / Don't

Do

  • Deep ink night base with 2-3 soft light wells
  • Colorless glass: bg-white/5 to bg-white/12 only
  • backdrop-blur-[40px]+ with backdrop-saturate-[180%]
  • Directional edge light: lit top inset + shaded bottom inset
  • 2-3% film grain overlay to remove the plastic feel
  • One accent — champagne #E4B863 on primary actions only

Don't

  • Purple-pink AI gradients (#667eea → #764ba2 → #f093fb)
  • Glass on flat solid backgrounds with nothing to refract
  • Glass opacity above 15% — it becomes a tinted block
  • Tinting the glass itself — color belongs to the scene
  • Low blur (backdrop-blur-sm) or missing saturate boost
  • Single-layer flat shadows without edge light
07Philosophy

Optics, not a color scheme

Colorless Glass

Real glass has no color of its own. Panels use only low-opacity white; every hue the eye reads comes from the scene behind.

Deep Night Scenes

A near-black ink base with soft light wells gives the glass something worth refracting — no loud gradients needed.

Light Has Direction

A lit top edge and a shaded bottom edge are what separate real glass from a translucent rectangle.

One Accent

Champagne gold appears only on primary actions, key numbers and highlights — never as a surface color.

Film Grain

A 2-3% noise overlay breaks up the digital smoothness and removes the plastic feel from large frosted areas.

Fluid Motion

Spring easing (cubic-bezier 0.16,1,0.3,1) simulates the physical inertia of glass. Nothing snaps, everything flows.
